Halloween is 2,000 years older than candy.

Before costumes and chocolate, it was a night for the dead. Here is how it became the Halloween we know.

Where it began

2,000 years of Halloween in four scenes.

About 2,000 years ago

Samhain fires

The Celts of Ireland, Britain and northern France celebrated Samhain (say "SOW-in"), the end of the harvest and the start of winter. They believed the dead could cross back on October 31, so people lit bonfires and wore disguises to hide from spirits.

The 8th century

A night gets its name

The church named November 1 All Saints' Day, also called All Hallows. The night before became All Hallows' Eve, later shortened to Hallowe'en.

The 1800s

Halloween crosses the ocean

Irish and Scottish immigrants brought the tradition with them to North America.

1927

Trick or treat, Alberta!

The words "trick or treat" first appeared in print in a Lethbridge Herald story about Halloween in Blackie, Alberta.

Made in Alberta

The first jack-o'-lantern was a turnip

Trick-or-treating has older cousins

In medieval Britain, "souling" meant going door to door and offering prayers for the dead in exchange for soul cakes. In Scotland and Ireland, children went "guising," performing a song or a joke for a treat.
